The flight's foot on the first floor: three ways to give it room

A house's hall from above, to scale (40 px a metre), the back wall at the top, the street at the bottom; 700 m house 60 (8.3 m deep, three storeys). Grey: walls. Hatched: the flight down (the opening). Brown: the next flight up, its first step marked. Green arrow: how a walker gets onto it.

Railings

Either way, the plastered half walls (along the flights' open sides and round the openings) can become wooden railings: an oak handrail at 0.9–1.0 m on square balusters 11 cm apart at most and a newel post at each end, in the doors' oak. They read lighter and open the corridor up; thin balusters can shimmer seen from far, but the stairs are only seen from inside, close.
