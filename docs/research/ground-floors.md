# Ground floors, shops and gardens in a Mediterranean city

*Research note, 2026-10-15, for the owner's note on the generator's ground floors (rez-de-chaussée
and rez-de-jardin, green shared courts, shared kitchen gardens, shops only where commerce goes).
Done by a research agent; the agent opened and checked most sources, and the claims seen only in
search results are marked so.*

Conventions:
- *(derived)* marks the agent's arithmetic on sourced numbers.
- *(estimate)* marks judgement with no source.
- "Search result only" means the figure was seen in a search result, but the page itself could
  not be opened.

Population bases for the derived figures: Paris ≈ 2.17 M (60 846 shops ÷ 28 per 1 000);
Barcelona ≈ 1.67 M (61 875 premises ÷ 3.7 per 100).

**Summary.**
- **Concentration:** shops crowd onto a few streets. Paris's protected commercial streets are 16 %
  of its streets but hold 53 % of its shops: one shop every 19 m a side there, against one every
  115 m elsewhere.
- **Clusters:** two thirds of French consumer outlets sit in clusters of at least 20 outlets
  within 200 m of each other.
- **A city of a million** would have:
  - about 26 000–30 000 active ground floors and 6–10 % vacancy;
  - one bakery in reach of nearly every home;
  - pharmacies at least 250 m apart;
  - supermarkets 400 m apart;
  - 25–40 markets;
  - no car-based malls.
- **Rez-de-jardin:** a ground-floor flat that opens level onto its own garden. Its garden,
  25–40 m², erases the ground floor's usual price discount.
- **Shared kitchen gardens:** raised beds 0.6–0.9 m high and level paths of 1.2–1.4 m, so a
  wheelchair user can garden too.
- **Patents:** none found on procedural placement of shops.

## 1. How retail spreads through European cities

### 1.1 The hierarchy

- **INSEE's equipment ranges.** INSEE's equipment base sorts services into three ranges
  ([INSEE BPE](https://www.insee.fr/fr/metadonnees/source/serie/s1161/presentation)):
  - "proximity": a bakery, a pharmacy, a post office, a nursery school;
  - "intermediate": a supermarket, a secondary school, the police;
  - "superior": a hypermarket, a lycée, a maternity hospital.
- **Daily against occasional shops.** French commercial planning places daily shops close to
  homes, "finely meshed", and groups the "exceptional" ones on a few sites so that people can
  compare them
  ([TCO DAAC diagnostic, 2018](https://www.tco.re/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/tco-daac_rapport_diag_v4-final.pdf)).
- **INSEE's commercial clusters** (2022 data;
  [INSEE Première 2091, Jan 2026](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/fichier/version-html/8730497/IP2091.pdf)):
  - Two thirds of consumer outlets sit in 6 700 clusters.
  - A cluster is at least **20 outlets that are neighbours within 200 m**.
  - It counts as a "centre" cluster when it meets two of three tests: near the town hall, more
    than 100 residents inside it, fewer than 8 staff per outlet.
  - A "periphery" cluster is at least 4 000 m² of sales area within 250 m.
  - The average cluster has 64 outlets and the median 27; one in ten has more than 106.
  - The two kinds compare like this:

    | Cluster | Outlets (mean) | Staff per outlet | Sales area per outlet |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Centre (47 % of the clusters) | 108 | 3 | 92 m² |
    | Periphery | 26 | 12 | 586 m² |
    | Outside any cluster | — | 3 | 172 m² |
- **Older data** (2015;
  [INSEE Première 1858](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/fichier/version-html/5386414/1858.pdf)):
  - Periphery clusters held 23 % of the outlets but 65 % of the floor area.
  - Small clusters (under 50 outlets) are more than 40 % food: neighbourhood parades are mostly
    food.
- **Marseille** calls itself about a hundred "village cores", a polycentric pattern that suits a
  Mediterranean city of a million (search result only:
  [Marseille 11-12](https://marseille1112.fr/sites/default/files/2024-04/22-044-vat.pdf)).

### 1.2 The share of ground floors that are shops

- **Paris.** APUR's BDCom is a census on foot every three years
  ([APUR BDCom](https://www.apur.org/en/open-data-maps/observatory/observatory-businesses-and-shops-paris-bdcom)).
  The 2023 figures
  ([paris.fr](https://www.paris.fr/pages/avec-28-commerces-pour-1000-habitants-paris-maintient-sa-densite-commerciale-a-un-haut-niveau-26008)):
  - **83 154 premises**, of which 60 846 are shops, cafés, restaurants or services: **28 per
    1 000 inhabitants**.
  - **10.9 % vacant.**
  - Streets with commercial protection are 313 km of 1 960 (16 %), but they hold about 53 % of
    the shops.
  - That makes about **one shop every 19 m a side there, against one every 115 m elsewhere**
    *(derived)*.
- **Barcelona.** The 2024 census counted 61 875 active ground-floor establishments: 3.7 per 100
  inhabitants, 90.96 % of the premises in use, 21 167 retail and 33 740 services
  ([Ajuntament](https://ajuntament.barcelona.cat/comerc/es/censo-comercial-2024)).
- **Marseille.** 24.95 shops per 1 000 in 2022 and 15 % vacancy in 2025 (search result only:
  Institut Montaigne). The Aix-Marseille Métropole calls vacancy over 8 % "problematic"
  ([AMP, 2018](https://deliberations.ampmetropole.fr/documents/metropole/deliberations/2018/12/13/DELIBERATION/D06UP.pdf)).
- **Lyon's PLU-H** protects commercial frontages over a **7 m depth** from the facade. It exempts
  the entrance hall, car and bike parking, the technical rooms and the caretaker's room
  ([data.gouv.fr](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/5e3aecae6f4441741c8cdd02)). So a commercial
  frontage still keeps its lobby and its service rooms.

### 1.3 Where shops cluster

- **Central streets.** In Barcelona, street centrality computed on the network alone correlates
  with where economic activities sit. Central arterials are "the cores, not the borders, of
  neighbourhoods"
  ([Porta et al., Urban Studies 2012](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/urbstu/v49y2012i7p1471-1488.html)).
- **Movement.** Hillier's "movement economy": the street grid sets pedestrian flows, and shops
  then multiply them (search result only).
- **Transit stops.** Shopfronts gather round central stations
  ([Rao & Pafka 2021](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/1568193-shopping-morphologies-of-urban-transit-station-areas--a-comparative-study-of-central-city-station-catchments-in-toronto--san-francisco--and-melbourne),
  abstract only).
- **Big hubs.** Paris's largest mall stands on its busiest station, Châtelet-Les Halles (search
  result only).
- **Tourism** protects centre shops. Between 2009 and 2015, employment fell in 29 % of centre
  clusters in tourist towns, against 39 % elsewhere (INSEE 1858).
- **Squares and boulevards** hold most of Paris's markets
  ([paris.fr](https://www.paris.fr/pages/les-marches-parisiens-2428)).
- **Waterfronts:** no quantitative study found; the waterfront figures below are *(estimates)*.

**Barcelona's rules on the distance between businesses of a type.**
- The Eixample's plan of 2022:
  - **25 m** between restaurants, self-service shops and convenience stores;
  - **200 m** between gyms, tourist services and bike shops;
  - **400 m** between supermarkets
    ([Ara](https://es.ara.cat/sociedad/colau-limita-bares-actividades-turisticas-eixample_1_4292803.html)).
- Ciutat Vella's plan of 2025: 50 m between self-service shops

## 2. Markets, malls and cultural venues

- **Markets.**
  - Paris has **83 food markets: 74 open-air and 9 covered**, about one per 26 000 residents
    *(derived)*. They are closed on Mondays and run 7:00 to 13:30
    ([paris.fr](https://www.paris.fr/pages/les-marches-parisiens-2428)).
  - Barcelona has **39 municipal market halls**, about one per 43 000 *(derived)*
    ([Ajuntament](https://ajuntament.barcelona.cat/comerc/ca/directori-de-comerc/mercats-municipals)).
- **Malls.**
  - French big-box retail lives in periphery clusters in non-residential zones (INSEE 2091), and
    it depends on car parks.
  - A car-free city instead has *(estimate)*: department stores in the centre, one or two malls
    on top of tram interchanges (the Les Halles model), and neighbourhood supermarkets 400 m
    apart.
- **Cinemas.**
  - Every French urban unit over 100 000 people has one
    ([Banque des Territoires](https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/le-nombre-de-salles-de-cinema-en-hausse-depuis-dix-ans)).
  - Paris has more than 400 screens, about 18 per 100 000 *(derived)*. They cluster in the centre:
    the 5th and 6th arrondissements alone have 27 cinemas and 70 screens
    ([paris.fr](https://www.paris.fr/pages/le-cinema-a-paris-2347)).
- **Live performance.**
  - France has 950 live-performance venues, concentrated in the big cities
    ([culture.gouv.fr](https://www.culture.gouv.fr/Media/Medias-creation-rapide/03-Approche-geographique-de-l-offre-culturelle.pdf)).
  - Barcelona has 30 theatres, about 18 per million people *(derived)*
    ([3cat](https://www.3cat.cat/3catinfo/una-temporada-teatral-de-record-els-3-milions-despectadors-cada-vegada-mes-a-prop/noticia-amp/3271047/)).

## 3. Ground floors that are not shops

- **Service rooms.** Lyon's exemptions list what every ground floor needs: a lobby, a bike room,
  technical rooms and a caretaker's room.
  - France's 2022 rule asks for 1.5 m² of bike parking per space: one space per flat of up to two
    rooms, two per larger flat (search result only).
  - Services outnumber retail on Barcelona's ground floors, 33 740 to 21 167.
- **Rez-de-jardin**
  ([Plan Immobilier](https://www.plan-immobilier.fr/actualites-immobilieres/quelle-difference-entre-rez-de-chaussee-et-rez-de-jardin)):
  - It is a ground-floor flat that opens level onto a garden; with only a court or a terrace, it
    stays a rez-de-chaussée.
  - The garden is usually a common part with exclusive private use. A garden of 25–40 m² can
    erase the ground floor's 10–25 % discount
    ([Home Select](https://homeselect.paris/blog/rez-de-chaussee-paris-guide/)).
  - Hedges over 2 m must stand 2 m from the boundary, lower plants 0.5 m
    ([art. 671 Code civil](https://www.construires.fr/distances-legales-plantations-art-671/)).
- **Privacy, as Paris residents asked for it**
  ([Paris workshop](https://cdn.paris.fr/paris/2020/02/26/970e2c242f03451626fb7fe0dbba006c.pdf)):
  low railings or low walls with planting rather than tall fences, a strip of green "to look at",
  and ground floors "protected from prying eyes".
  - A raised ground floor gives privacy but breaks the wheelchair-first rule. The step-free
    alternatives are depth and planting: Den Haag allows a 45 cm facade garden as long as 1.5 m
    of pavement stays clear
    ([denhaag.nl](https://www.denhaag.nl/nl/in-de-stad/natuur-en-milieu/gevel-en-boomtuintjes.htm)).
- **Green courts.**
  - Barcelona has reopened 45 Eixample block interiors as gardens, aiming at green space within
    200 m of every home
    ([Ajuntament](https://ajuntament.barcelona.cat/eixample/ca/coneixeu-el-districte/interiors-dilla)).
  - French accessible terraces keep their threshold at 2 cm or less: pedestal slabs, gratings,
    low-sill doors
    ([FFB](https://www.ffbatiment.fr/revues-guides/guides/carnet-de-details-pour-l-accessibilite-des-balcons,-des-loggias-et-des-terrasses-dans-les-constructi)).
- **Shared kitchen gardens.**
  - Paris had 154 shared gardens in 2025, typically 300–400 m² (search results only).
  - The "Main Verte" charter: open to the public at least two half-days a week, composting,
    rainwater collection, no pesticides
    ([charter](https://cdn.paris.fr/paris/2025/09/16/mv_charte_a4_2024-x8l7.pdf)).
  - An accessible garden in Mably: raised beds 0.60 m high for seated gardening and 0.90 m for
    standing, 1.20 m paths, tables 0.60 m wide from one side or 1.20 m from both
    ([Banque des Territoires](https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/handicap-mably-42-le-jardin-est-un-loisir-pour-tous)).

## 4. Recommendations for the generator

### 4.1 City-wide targets for a million people

| Item | Target |
|---|---|
| Active commercial ground floors | 26 000–30 000 |
| Vacant units | 6–10 % |
| Food shops | about 3 800, of which 550–700 bakeries |
| Cafés, bars, restaurants | 7 000–9 000 |
| Pharmacies | 360–420 |
| Food markets | 25–40, of which 8–15 covered halls |
| Supermarkets | 120–200 *(estimate)* |
| Cinemas | 15–25, with 100–180 screens |
| Theatres | 15–25 |
| Car-based malls | none; 1–2 malls on top of tram hubs |

### 4.2 The chance that a street ground floor is active *(estimates)*

Calibrated on Paris's one shop every 19 m on protected streets against one every 115 m elsewhere.

| Frontage | Chance | Cafés and restaurants among them |
|---|---|---|
| Centre high street, central tram avenue | 0.85–0.95 | 25–30 % |
| District high street (tram avenue) | 0.55–0.75 | 20–25 % |
| Neighbourhood parade (a stretch of 80–200 m) | 0.5–0.7 | 15–20 %, mostly food |
| Facing a square | 0.9 in the centre, 0.6 elsewhere | 40–50 % |
| On a sea or river promenade | 0.8 in the centre, 0.4 elsewhere | 50–60 % |
| Ordinary pedestrian street | 0.05–0.12 | corner cafés |
| Green court edge | 0 for shops | — |

### 4.3 Modifiers *(estimates)*

- **Distance to the centre:** ×1.0 within 1 km, ×0.8 at 3 km, ×0.6 beyond 5 km.
- **Tram stop:** +0.15 within 100 m, +0.07 within 250 m.
- **Corner plot:** +0.10.
- **A sight within 200 m:** +0.15.
- **Facing a market hall:** +0.2.
- Clamp the result between 0 and 0.95.

### 4.4 Spacing

- **Bakeries:** every home within 350 m of one.
- **Pharmacies:** at least 250 m apart.
- **Supermarkets:** at least 400 m apart.
- **Gyms and bike shops:** at least 200 m apart.
- **Restaurants in residential streets:** at least 25 m apart.
- **Markets:** every home within about 1 km of one.

### 4.5 A check: INSEE's own cluster definition

Run INSEE's definition (20 outlets within 200 m) on the generated city. Expect *(estimate)*: one
central cluster of 5 000–8 000 outlets, 10–15 district clusters of 150–500, 60–120 parades of
20–60, and 15–25 % of outlets isolated.

### 4.6 Ground floors that are not shops *(estimates)*

- **In every building:** one lobby per stair core on the street, a bike room by the lobby, a bin
  room near the street, the meters.
- **On a non-commercial street front:**
  - 60–75 % flats, set back behind a planted strip 1.5–3 m deep with low railings, and never
    raised;
  - 10–15 % practices and offices;
  - 5–10 % vacant.
- **On the court side:** flats only.

### 4.7 Garden flats *(estimates, after Home Select's sizes)*

- **Who gets one:** on green-court blocks, 40–60 % of the court-side ground-floor flats get a
  private garden; the rest get a terrace at least 1.5 m deep with planters.
- **Size:** 25–40 m², the flat's width by 4–6 m deep.
- **Edges:** a hedge under 2 m, 0.5 m inside the boundary, between neighbours; a low railing with
  a step-free gate onto the court.
- **Opening:** large glazed sliding bays onto a terrace 2.5–3 m deep, the threshold at 2 cm or
  less.
- **The street side** never gets private gardens, only a planted buffer.

### 4.8 Shared kitchen gardens *(estimates)*

- **Where and how big:** one in each green court, or one for 2–3 blocks, in the court's sunniest
  part; 100–400 m².
- **Beds:** raised 0.6–0.7 m (a few at 0.9 m), 0.6 m wide from one side or 1.2 m from both.
- **Paths:** level, firm, 1.2–1.4 m wide, at most 5 %.
- **Fittings:** a tap, compost bins, a tool shed, a bench and a table, a step-free gate.

### 4.9 Venues *(estimates)*

- **Markets:** covered halls on district squares by a tram stop; open-air markets on boulevards
  and squares.
- **Culture:** cinemas in the centre and the district clusters; theatres facing the central
  squares; museums by the sights.
- **Libraries:** one per district.
